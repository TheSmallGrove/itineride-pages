how it works

Three moves, and the itinerary is done.

Nothing to set up before you start: open the map, and the first tap is already a stop.

Drop the stops

Tap the map, search for a place or paste a pair of coordinates: every point becomes a stop. Choose one way or round trip — the two legs stay separate, each with its own stops — and pin passes, refuges and viewpoints as points of interest, without bending the route to reach them.

Route every leg on its own

Each leg is calculated separately and comes back with distance, duration, average speed and turn-by-turn directions. Motorways and tolls are a switch you flip per leg — that is how the good roads get picked, and the dull transfer stays a transfer.

Take it with you

The summary collects the totals of the trip and splits them day by day, rest days included. Share the itinerary with the people riding with you, export it as GPX, or hand it over to Google Maps the morning you leave.

what is inside

Everything a trip needs. Nothing it doesn't.

No feed, no friends to add, no cloud to sync. A planner that opens fast and keeps what you wrote.

Stops, however you find them

A tap on the map, a search by name, or a pair of coordinates pasted from a forum post. All three end up as the same thing: a stop on your route.

One way or round trip

Pick round trip and the return becomes its own leg, with its own stops and its own roads. Coming home never has to retrace the way out.

Points of interest

A pass, a refuge, a viewpoint you want to remember: pin it along the way. It shows on the map without changing a metre of the route.

Favourites

The places you keep coming back to stay one tap away, ready to drop into the next trip you start.

Motorways and tolls, per leg

Two switches on every single leg. Fly down the motorway to reach the mountains, then keep the passes clear of them.

Day by day, rest days included

The totals split across the days you will actually ride, so a week away stops being one long number.

GPX in and out

Export a single track or the whole trip — notes, links, stop types and rest days included — and open a GPX somebody shared with you.

Everything stays on the device

A local archive: no account, no sign-up, no server of ours in the middle. Nothing to delete later, because nothing was ever sent.

Your units, your language

Kilometres or miles, and the whole app — turn instructions and place names included — in English, Italian, French, Spanish or German.

phone & tablet

Before you ride

itineride plans, it does not navigate: on the road the itinerary is handed over to Google Maps. Routes are calculated with OpenRouteService, and on first launch the app asks for your own personal key for that service — it is free and takes a few minutes to get from their site. It is also the reason no account is needed here: the calculations go through your key, not through our servers.
